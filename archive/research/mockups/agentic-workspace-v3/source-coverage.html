32 patterns. One considered interface.

All 32 UI/UX pattern pages and the two supplied articles were read on 9 October 2026. No unresolved access failures. Three narrowly scoped research agents covered disjoint sets to reduce repeated work.

This is page-by-page text research. The primary agent additionally inspected rendered screenshots and computed styles of the Agentic Design category page and UX Pilot article. Interactive demos and every rendered subpage were not visually exercised. Guidance below is paraphrased; the design implications are project judgments, not claims of implemented capability.

Source / access Source guidance Design implication Condition or pitfall
Human-in-the-Loop — accessed Put human judgment at consequential or ambiguous decision points; route reviews to appropriate people and capture decisions. Use a decision card with the proposed action, affected record, impact, reviewer and clear approval/rejection controls. Keep review items discoverable after leaving the conversation. Source: over-escalation creates bottlenecks and rushed reviews. Application condition: thresholds never replace required explicit approval.
Human On the Loop — accessed Supervise bounded autonomous work with exception alerts, context and readily available intervention. During authorized background preparation, show current work and a supported stop/pause control; surface exceptions in the same task view. Source: oversight fails when people cannot realistically respond, or alerts overload attention. Do not substitute supervision for required active approval.
Progressive Disclosure — accessed Present essential information first, with consistent expansion controls and clear typography; remember useful disclosure preferences. Keep the result and next action visible. Put source excerpts and activity history in separately labeled expandable sections, using one consistent chevron component. Source: critical information must not be buried, and deep nesting impedes use. Inference: never hide action scope or material uncertainty behind a disclosure; show evidence summaries rather than hidden model reasoning.
Confidence Visualization — accessed Explain uncertainty and its drivers, calibrate against actual correctness, and avoid false numerical precision or confidence as the only decision factor. Prefer factual evidence labels such as “Source unavailable,” “Needs confirmation,” and “Checked against record.” Do not add a decorative percentage confidence gauge. Source: normal uncertainty should not look like an emergency; deterministic outputs do not need model confidence. Inference: numerical confidence requires an actual calibrated basis, which this fixture does not establish.
Mixed-Initiative Interfaces — accessed Make human/AI ownership and handoffs visible, present suggestions as editable proposals, and provide ways to regain control. Allow review and revision within the artifact card; use explicit labels such as “Your review” and “Preparing draft” at transitions. Source: unexpected takeover and rigid collaboration frustrate users. Inference: a changed action becomes a fresh proposal and cannot inherit approval silently.
Agent Status & Activity — accessed Describe the current activity, use consistent state indicators, and offer timestamped history; avoid generic loading messages and flickering states. Use one compact status row with icon plus text: “Checking source records,” “Waiting for review,” or “Verifying result.” Expand history on demand. Source: too many simultaneous indicators overwhelm. Inference: show percentages or time estimates only when supported; distinguish completed dispatch from independently verified outcome.
Monitoring and Control — accessed Use summary-to-detail hierarchy, exception-based alerts, accessible controls and an audit trail of interventions. Put active tasks and review requests in a small work list, with context-specific controls in each task. Source: comprehensive mission-control dashboards are unsuitable for simple agents/prototypes. Inference: do not inflate the landing page with operational charts or imply that stopping reverses an already committed effect.
Error Handling and Recovery — accessed Explain the problem, known cause and next step; preserve user work and reveal technical detail only when useful. Use an inline recovery card beside the preserved draft. An unknown write outcome should offer “Check outcome,” while missing access should show the relevant access path. Source: generic advice, silent failure and unnecessary modal dialogs impede recovery. Repository condition: the source's generic retry advice cannot justify replaying a possibly committed write.
Approval Queues & Escalation Chains — accessed Treat approval as a workflow with contextual review packages, asynchronous waiting, explicit escalation signals and preserved state. A review queue item should carry owner, waiting time, action summary and impact; reopening it restores the exact proposal and highlights stale context. Source: delayed reviews can resume with drifted or incomplete context. Repository condition: do not adopt the page's auto-approval or privileged-agent adjudication suggestions where explicit human approval is required; revalidate before execution.
Trust and Transparency — accessed Attribute sources, distinguish generated material and explain consequential decisions at suitable depth; transparency should support understanding and action. Mark generated drafts clearly and attach source excerpts and a concise decision summary to the artifact; link verification evidence to the outcome. Source: excessive detail and explanations without actionable information undermine usefulness. Inference: show provenance and concise rationale, never hidden chain-of-thought or a trust badge unsupported by evidence.
Accessibility in Agent Design — accessed Design keyboard and assistive-technology support from the start, use semantic controls, simplify language, and avoid color-only meaning. Pair status colors with text/icons; keep visible focus, meaningful button labels, accessible disclosure controls and stable focus during updates. Source: one accommodation cannot serve every disability, and overlays are insufficient. Inference: retain these basics in the prototype despite the page's “avoid” suggestions for internal demos; automated checks alone do not establish accessibility.
Conversational Interface Patterns — accessed Support natural multi-step exchanges, recoverable interruptions, progressive disclosure, and context-sensitive communication. Use clear machine language. Keep a concise conversation beside a structured task artifact; label activity “Checking sources” or “Preparing draft.” Endless scrolling chat is unsuitable for every task; proactive interruptions can become intrusive.
Chat Interface Patterns — accessed Preserve topic context and expose rich content, citations, and expandable details. Give each task a resumable conversation and source drawer; use reusable document and action cards. Branching, presence, and reactions increase cognitive load; the page advises against full complexity for minimal or single-user needs.
Agent Collaboration UX — accessed Preserve context during handoffs; show responsibilities, activity, and human intervention points with consistent indicators. A compact activity row can state who is handling the current step and why a handoff occurred, with details on demand. Do not make users repeat context; coordination overhead is unjustified for simple tasks. Multi-step work alone does not establish a need for multiple agents in e-agent.
Adaptive Interface Patterns — accessed Begin with explicit preferences, explain adaptations, allow overrides, and retain core functionality. Let users choose compact or expanded assistant mode and retain that choice within the study. Frequent unexplained layout changes undermine stability; regulated or time-critical contexts may need consistency.
Context Window Management UI — accessed Warn before capacity limits, let users preserve important context, and make summarization reversible. Show a plain-language context summary with attached records/sources; surface capacity details only when they affect a decision. Silent removal or compression can lose essential facts. A permanent token meter is unnecessary for short tasks.
Onboarding and Education Patterns — accessed Introduce boundaries progressively through practice, immediate feedback, and contextual help. Use a small set of realistic starter tasks with an explicit sample label and brief explanation of when approval is required. Avoid long mandatory tours or promises beyond actual capability; the page explicitly limits heavy onboarding for temporary prototypes.
Ambient Agent Patterns — accessed Maintain background state and request attention selectively through notifications, questions, or reviews. Keep a quiet task inbox with an actionable count; reserve interruption for a decision or failure that needs the user. Excess alerts create fatigue; background sensing and unpredictable actions can feel invasive. A mockup must not imply real continuous monitoring.
Cross-Platform Agent UX — accessed after retry Share a design system while adapting interactions and layout to the platform; preserve task context across transitions. Use one icon, type, spacing, and component system; collapse the assistant into a focused mobile surface while keeping task identity visible. Forcing the desktop layout onto mobile harms usability. Real device synchronization needs implementation beyond responsive styling.
Visual Reasoning Patterns — accessed Reveal workflow progress, sources, uncertainty, and decision factors progressively; make visualizations functional. Use an expandable activity/evidence timeline with observed checks and concise decision summaries. Dense diagrams obscure understanding, and numerical confidence can be mistaken for a guarantee. e-agent must not display hidden chain-of-thought or fabricated scores.
Multimodal Interaction Patterns — accessed Keep meaning consistent across input modes, support transitions and fallback, and respect environmental/accessibility needs. Keep text and keyboard operation complete; add attachment or voice affordances only when the demonstrated workflow supports them. Too many simultaneous channels overwhelm; privacy, latency, and synchronization can make multimodal interaction inappropriate.
Generative UI — accessed Match interactive presentation to intent/content: forms for actions, tables for comparison, charts for trends; isolate generated rendering. Use a small approved library of structured artifact components inside a stable shell, with host-owned approval controls. This is an adaptation, not a claim of runtime-generated UI. Rendering and compatibility failures compound agent errors; dynamically generated content requires testing and containment.
Privacy and Security UX — accessed Explain data use plainly; offer discoverable, granular permissions and access/deletion controls. Place a short “Data access” summary beside a task's connected sources, with details on demand. Attractive controls are misleading unless downstream access actually respects them.
Privacy-by-Design Principles — accessed Make privacy protective defaults, data minimization, purpose limits, and lifecycle protection architectural concerns. Default task context to explicitly selected sources and show their purpose before execution. Incomplete threat modeling leaves gaps; privacy is costly to retrofit.
Granular Privacy Controls — accessed Separate permissions by data type, purpose, sharing context, and retention; enable revocation. Use a compact source-permission list with plain scope labels, rather than one “Allow everything” toggle. People may grant broad access without reading; granularity alone does not create informed choices.
Progressive Consent & Communication — accessed Ask at the point of need, explain risks briefly, and reveal detailed terms progressively. Request new access inline when a task first needs it, with one short explanation and expandable scope. Repetitive requests still cause consent fatigue.
Transparent Data Handling — accessed Show understandable data processing, storage, sharing, and access activity. Let a task's evidence drawer explain which source was read and how it contributed to the result. Excessive technical detail overwhelms; excessive simplification conceals material risks.
User Empowerment Privacy Dashboard — accessed Centralize visibility and controls for personal data across agents, including export and deletion. Keep a single secondary “Data & access” settings page with source inventory and request status. Heterogeneous backends can leave stale copies or incomplete deletions; UI completion must reflect actual results.
Regulatory Compliance UX — accessed Make explanations, rights requests, consent, and audit evidence understandable in context. Expose a concise “Why this needs review” explanation with evidence in approval details. Disclosure walls and ceremonial compliance checkboxes can obscure decisions.
Advanced Privacy Technologies UX — accessed Explain privacy technology guarantees in ordinary language, with verifiable supporting detail. If a supported protection is displayed, explain its actual scope through an adjacent details link. Simplified explanations can imply guarantees stronger than the technology provides.
Realtime Voice Agents — accessed Spoken interactions need clear turn taking, interruption, tool-call handling, guardrails, and handoff. Omit voice from the primary procurement workspace unless a concrete voice workflow is established; any later voice control needs visible listening/stopped states. Tool latency, network loss, and voice activity detection errors disrupt conversation.
AG-UI Protocol — accessed Structured lifecycle, activity, and state events let frontends render live progress and return user controls. Use stable task states and a compact activity history; visibly distinguish reconnecting/stale status from completed work. Streaming requires ordering, replay, and state reconciliation. This pattern alone does not warrant a protocol migration.
UX Pilot: 8 Enterprise UX Design Best Practices and Principles — accessed Research distinct roles; maintain reusable components; embed help and AI suggestions in workflows; support role-aware data and measure task efficiency and errors. Build the workspace around a focused work list and selected task, sharing one table, button, badge, field, and icon system across views. Dense workflows need learnability; abbreviated labels and icon-only actions can confuse new users.
Alexandra Vasquez: Agentic UX—7 principles — accessed Establish consistent information architecture, embed agents in existing workflows, offer useful next steps, maintain context, reuse familiar patterns, gather timely inputs, and preserve human control. Put proposed action, explanation, review controls, and resulting state beside the affected task instead of a separate AI destination. Separate AI panels create context switching; automation must remain visible and appropriately reversible.
