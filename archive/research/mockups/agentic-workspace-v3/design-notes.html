Make the work clear.
Make the decision human.

An enterprise agent should help people understand and advance a task. This concept puts the outcome, source context, and approval boundary into a familiar workspace and an assistant that can stay inside another app.

All organizations, identities, data, integrations, and workflow results in this study are fictional. The six scenarios illustrate a product direction, not e-agent’s currently implemented integration coverage.

How the references shaped the mockup

01 · TASKS BEYOND CHAT

Intent, artifact, decision, result

A chat message sets the task. A document or action card makes the proposed outcome inspectable. Status and recovery are visible outside the conversation.

02 · CONTEXT THAT STAYS

Keep sources close to the work

Minimizing or expanding preserves the task. Source excerpts open in a focused dialog and return to the review. Rich artifacts provide structure without introducing branching conversations.

Agentic Design · Chat Interface Patterns ↗

03 · ASSISTANCE IN PLACE

No mandatory trip to an AI screen

The customer record includes “Prepare delivery handoff.” The assistant appears with that context. It asks for missing information at the point it matters and keeps the person in control.

04 · ENTERPRISE CLARITY

Predictable navigation and components

The workspace separates work from connection setup. Consistent cards, labels, spacing, and disclosure patterns serve several domains. The People example explains missing access instead of pretending to complete a task.

Pattern Concrete design decision Guardrail
Human in the loop Review the artifact, then explicitly confirm the exact action. Approving a document cannot also approve a message.
Progressive disclosure Summary and material terms first; sources and audit details on demand. Scope and material terms precede content and are repeated in confirmation. Warnings stay visible; full content remains scrollable.
Agent status and activity Working, needs input, needs review, verifying, verified, and unknown are distinct. No invented completion percentage or confidence score.
Trust and transparency Source excerpts, revision labels, acting identity, and action scope. Explanations use visible evidence, not hidden model reasoning.
Error recovery Unknown effects offer a read-back check; stale proposals require a fresh review. An unresolved check stays unresolved. No blind retry.
Mixed initiative The assistant prepares; the user clarifies, inspects, rejects, or approves. Authority is never inferred from conversational text.
Ambient assistance Contextual launch from a customer record, with minimize and resume. The prototype reads only its fictional fixture context.

A restrained visual system

CanvasSurfaceActionVerifiedAttention

System sans-serif for controls and information. A serif document heading distinguishes the artifact from the assistant. Line icons, quiet borders, modest elevation, text-labelled status, visible keyboard focus, and reduced-motion support keep the visual language consistent.

Try these flows

  1. Open Prepare a customer handoff. Expand the assistant and inspect a source.
  2. Review and approve the document. Simulate its read-back. Observe the separate message approval.
  3. Choose Unknown outcome in the study bar. Check the existing result; it remains unresolved.
  4. Choose Needs input, select an audience, and return to the updated review.
  5. Try Prepare an onboarding plan to see the missing-connection state.
  6. Select Try inside an app, then launch the handoff directly from the customer record.

What this does not establish

This is a reviewable interaction prototype. It does not demonstrate live integration coverage, permission enforcement, enterprise readiness, or usability improvement. Role research and task-based evaluation with real users remain necessary. The existing e-agent approval and verification contracts remain the implementation baseline.

The original repository research reports 12–14 remain unchanged. Earlier application edits were paused when the user requested this mockup first. This concept has not been applied to production components.
