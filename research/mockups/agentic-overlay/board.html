03 / The integration contract behind the experience

Business capability→Admitted provider binding→Scoped connection→Authorized action→Verified result

A domain defines the task’s semantics. A provider implements a capability. A connection supplies authorized scope and identity. One provider can serve many domains; one capability can have multiple valid providers. The UI renders admitted metadata. It does not choose credentials or implement business policy.

04 / Technologies: use each at its own boundary

RETAIN

Existing product UI and controller

React, React Aria, design tokens, typed API and durable SSE. Reuse the approval state machine and immutable source projections when implementing this direction.

INTEGRATE SELECTIVELY

Native APIs and admitted MCP tools

Both need capability mapping, identity, policy, recovery and outcome verification. MCP connectivity does not establish a correct business transaction.

TRACK, NOT REQUIRED

AG-UI and A2UI

Event interoperability and declarative display are separate concerns. Neither replaces the ledger or authorizes writes; generated approval surfaces remain excluded.

05 / Visual direction and usability questions

A neutral workspace with restrained lavender, open typography and app identity supplied as metadata. Calm remains the starting direction. AI Presence and Operator remain available in the task’s appearance menu, independently of floating, docked and expanded placement.

Can people discover useful work?

Ask users in sales, IT and people operations to find a task without starting from a provider or procurement vocabulary.

Can they explain a boundary?

Ask who will see a posted message, which identity acts, and why a searchable app may not support a write.

Can they handle an incomplete result?

Check whether users retain the verified document, avoid repeating an uncertain message, and recognize missing employee facts.
