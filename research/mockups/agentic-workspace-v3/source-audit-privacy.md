## Synthesis for the mockup

These are design inferences, constrained by the user's simple professional direction and repository integrity rules:

1. **Make the task the center.** Show a concise work list, task summary, current state, and one relevant next action. Keep activity and evidence available within that context. Privacy does not require another primary dashboard.
2. **Use one visual language.** A warm neutral canvas, navy text/primary actions, restrained semantic color, and one consistent line-icon family should extend through logo, navigation, controls, badges, and empty states. The palette is the user's direction; source articles support consistency and restrained visual hierarchy rather than these exact colors.
3. **Keep review specific.** Present proposed effect, scope, relevant evidence, and approval together. A privacy permission and a business-action approval are distinct decisions; neither should imply authority for the other.
4. **Reveal detail where it helps.** Default to a readable summary with explicit labels. Put source access, timestamps, provenance, and technical activity behind contextual disclosure rather than filling every screen with diagnostic panels.
5. **Promise only supported behavior.** A mockup must label demonstrations honestly. Do not imply deployed privacy enforcement, legal compliance, guaranteed deletion, cryptographic protections, voice support, undo of external writes, or live release qualification. A stale/unknown external outcome needs an explicit state and a safe reconciliation path, not a decorative success badge.

No application files were edited. No backend capabilities or new protocol adoption are proposed by this audit.
